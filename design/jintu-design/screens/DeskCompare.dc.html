Data analyst vs business analyst vs product analyst vs analytics engineer

One degree, one set of job adverts, four genuinely different jobs.

The short answer
A data analyst answers questions with data that exists. A business analyst works out what the business needs and writes it down, often with no SQL at all. A product analyst is a data analyst embedded in one product, owning its metrics. An analytics engineer builds the tested, documented models the other three query.

How to spot one
Data analyst
Answering questions. The number, and the caveats around it.
The pipeline, the model, or usually the decision.
Lives in SQL and a spreadsheet.
Business analyst
Requirements, process and stakeholder agreement.
Frequently, any data at all. Many BA roles involve no SQL.
Lives in documents and meetings.
Product analyst
One product's metrics, funnels, retention and experiments.
What to build. That is the PM's.
Is the one who says the experiment was not significant.
Analytics engineer
The transformation layer: models, definitions, tests, documentation.
The analysis itself, and not the ingestion either.
Writes SQL that other people's dashboards depend on.
Data engineer
Getting data in and keeping it flowing. Pipelines, orchestration, reliability.
What the numbers mean. Rarely does analysis.
Gets paged when a job fails at 3am.
Data scientist
Statistical modelling and inference, sometimes production models.
Reporting, in a well-staffed team. In India the title often means 'analyst with Python'.
Read the responsibilities, not the header.

Where people go wrong

The business analyst is the odd one out and the source of most disappointment. Candidates apply expecting to work with data and find the job is interviews, process maps and requirements. That is a good job, but it is a different one, and the overlap in title costs people years. If the advert does not mention SQL, assume there is none.

Analytics engineer is the role with the highest demand and the lowest awareness, which makes it unusually reachable. It is not a promotion from analyst: it is a sideways move into building the layer rather than using it, and plenty of excellent analysts dislike it.

Between data engineer and analytics engineer, the split is roughly: getting the data to land is engineering, making it mean something is analytics engineering.
