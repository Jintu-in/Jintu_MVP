# Jintu v3 — the open platform
### No cohorts. Login, learn anything, earn verified points, build curricula, share.

> Supersedes the cohort model in ARCHITECTURE.md and TRACK_MODEL.md. Keeps the verification engine, which becomes more important rather than less.

---

## The decision and what it costs

**Chosen:** free, open, self-paced, community-driven. Anyone logs in, learns anything, earns points, builds and shares curricula.

**Precedent:** PhysicsWallah — free audience first, monetize years later. Fits India's near-zero consumer willingness-to-pay for learning subscriptions, and fits a solo founder's real capacity. Cohorts were an operations business disguised as software.

**Price paid:** no revenue near-term, and no deadlines. Self-paced completion runs 5–15% against 65–85% for cohorts. That gap is real and no feature closes it fully.

**Consequence nobody expects:** cohorts *were* the verification layer. Removing them makes machine grading the entire integrity story.

---

## The one problem that must be solved

**If points can be earned by clicking, the platform is worthless.**

Not "less good" — worthless. A profile that can't distinguish someone who did the work from someone who scrolled is noise, and once it's noise the community has nothing to be built around. Every dead badge system on the internet died this way.

So the rule that replaces the cohort rule:

> **No point is ever awarded for consumption. Only for a submission that something checked.**

Marking a video watched earns nothing. Opening a track earns nothing. Time on site earns nothing. Submitting a query that runs correctly earns something.

This is stricter than the cohort model was, because there's no peer group to catch fakes.

## Tiers, renamed for an open platform

"Sprint" implied a cohort. Rename around **verification strength**, which is the only thing that now differs:

| Tier | Who builds it | Verification | Badge on profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Verified** | Jintu | Deterministic checks + answer keys + async peer + spot-audit | "Verified" — the strong one |
| **Community** | Anyone | Structural checks + async peer review | "Community-reviewed" |
| **Draft** | Generated once, cached | None | No points, no badge |

Same three tiers, honest new names. A learner sees immediately why one badge is worth more.

## Accountability without deadlines

Four mechanisms, all free to run, replacing the cohort:

**1. Streaks with freezes.** Daily verified reps. Two freezes a month, auto-applied — power cuts, exams, festivals. Duolingo's entire retention engine is this.

**2. Public commitment.** Pick a track, set your own target date, and it appears on your public profile. Self-set deadlines with an audience are weaker than cohort deadlines but far better than nothing.

**3. Review-to-unlock.** Your submission enters the review queue only after you've reviewed two others. This is how async peer review actually happens without a cohort forcing it — you need the queue, so you feed the queue. Coursera has run this for years.

**4. Visible progress on a shared track.** Anyone working the same track sees the others: how far along, last active. Not a leaderboard by points (which rewards grinding) — an ordering by artifacts shipped.

None of these match a cohort. Together they're a real mechanism rather than a hope.

## The points system, revised

Two ledgers survive unchanged in principle, with one addition.

```sql
point_events(
  id, user_id,
  ledger text check (ledger in ('consistency','proof')),
  source_type text check (source_type in ('daily_rep','artifact','peer_review')),
  source_id, points int,
  verification text not null,   -- which archetype awarded it. NEVER null.
  awarded_at, voided_at
)
```

**The new column is `verification`.** Every point records how it was earned. That makes the profile auditable: a recruiter or a college can see that 34 of someone's 41 points came from deterministic checks rather than from peers being nice.

| | Consistency | Proof |
|---|---|---|
| From | Daily verified reps, completed reviews | Graded artifacts only |
| Shown as | "41-day streak" | Scores, artifacts, badge |
| Feeds readiness score | **Never** | Yes |
| Buys | Streak status, review priority, profile flair | The credential |

Anti-gaming, all four still required, plus one new:

1. Daily consistency cap — 30 points max per calendar day
2. Reps are day-stamped, never backfillable
3. Peer reviews voided retroactively if a spot-audit finds them low-effort
4. Proof points locked until your own two reviews are submitted
5. **New: reciprocal-review detection.** Two accounts reviewing each other repeatedly get flagged. Without a cohort assigning reviewers, collusion is the obvious attack

## Monetization, later — three real options

You need none of these for 18 months. Ranked by fit:

**1. Pay to verify (best fit).** Everything free. Learning free, points free, profile free. Pay ₹499 once for a **verified credential** — your artifacts audited by a human, a signed certificate, a verification badge and a permanent verified URL. Preserves the free-community ethos and charges only for the one thing with real external value. No subscription, no auto-debit, no refund churn.

**2. Colleges.** The TPO dashboard returns. A college pays per student per year to see readiness across a batch and to have credentials verified in bulk. This is the largest realistic revenue line, and the platform being free is what gets students on it in the first place.

**3. Employers.** Search verified profiles by demonstrated skill. Only works at real scale, but it's the highest-margin version and the natural endpoint.

**Explicitly not:** consumer subscriptions (India's ARPU makes this futile), ads (destroys the trust the whole thing rests on), or paywalling curricula (contradicts the entire positioning).

## What I'd watch closely

**The cold-start problem is now your main risk, not completion.** An open platform with 12 users has no community, no review queue, and no reason to return. Review-to-unlock deadlocks at low volume — the first hundred submissions will need you reviewing them personally. Budget for that and don't be surprised by it.

**Deterministic verification is now a hard dependency, not an optimization.** Every Verified track needs an answer key or a runner before it can award a single point. This means fewer tracks, built more slowly, and you should resist the pull toward breadth until the first one is airtight.

**The 19 existing tracks are now a liability in a new way.** Previously they were unfinished courses. Now they'd be point-awarding tracks with no verification behind them, which would poison the credential from day one. Demote all but one to Draft immediately.

**You still need the policy pages.** Keep Terms, Refunds, Contact, Privacy. DPDP applies regardless of whether money changes hands, and 18+ gating still matters because readiness scoring is still profiling.

## One question I'd want answered before Phase 1

If nobody pays for eighteen months, what funds it, and what's your own honest tolerance for building something with no revenue signal for that long?

Not a rhetorical question. The cohort model gave you a brutal but fast answer — twenty people either pay ₹499 or they don't, and you know in three weeks. The open platform gives you no such signal for a long time, and the thing that kills free-first products isn't usually the market. It's the founder losing conviction in month nine with 400 signups and no revenue.

Worth deciding now, while you're energised, what number at what date would make you keep going — and what would make you stop.
